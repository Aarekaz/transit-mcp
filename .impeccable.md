## Design Context

### Users

Metro MCP serves people connecting Claude, Codex, ChatGPT, or another MCP
client to live DC Metro and NYC Subway data. The legal and support pages also
serve prospective users evaluating the service before connecting, existing
users troubleshooting a connection, security researchers, contributors, and
operators. Their jobs are to understand what the anonymous service does with
requests, what limits and disclaimers apply, and where to get help or report a
problem without hunting through repository internals.

### Brand Personality

Clear, civic, and dependable. Metro MCP should feel like a well-run public
transit information surface: direct enough for a developer, calm enough for a
non-specialist, and candid about live-data and edge-platform limitations. The
emotional goal is informed confidence, not legal intimidation or marketing
hype.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use the existing Metro MCP light visual language: warm off-white background,
deep transit navy text, orange route-like accent, crisp dividers, restrained
rounded geometry, and the established UI/monospace typography roles. Legal
pages should be editorial and highly readable, with a narrow reading measure,
strong section rhythm, a compact shared header/footer, and one understated
transit-line motif. They must be static and scriptless, with no external fonts,
trackers, forms, animation, browser storage, or decorative card grid. Mobile is
an adapted single reading column, not a shrunken desktop page.

### Design Principles

1. Put the answer before the caveat: summarize the page's promise in plain
   language, then provide precise operational detail.
2. Make trust inspectable: use canonical links, honest data-flow wording, and
   visible last-updated information; never imply guarantees the code or
   providers cannot make.
3. Preserve the Metro MCP family resemblance without redesigning the landing
   page or importing a new visual system.
4. Optimize for reading and keyboard navigation at 320px through desktop;
   maintain visible focus, semantic landmarks, sufficient contrast, and no
   horizontal overflow.
5. Keep the pages inert: every byte should serve content, navigation, or
   accessibility, with no client-side behavior or data collection.
